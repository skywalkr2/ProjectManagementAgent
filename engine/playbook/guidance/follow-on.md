Practice for checking what is tied to a date that moved or a fact that was replaced (core playbook):

- Follow the link first. An item that names the calendar entry (or its series) belongs to it, so what moved for the entry
  moved for the item. Words that match an old day are weaker: check that the record is about the same thing before
  asking about it.
- A decided date stays decided. Never change an action's day or words, and never move one with the meeting: ask the
  action's owner whether the date should change, or the person when nobody is named.
- Ask what the date was for. Work due on the entry's day, or between the old day and the new one, may now be early,
  late or fine; say what moved and let the one who decides say.
- A meeting that carried more than one dated action (a topic to cover, a plan, a set of items) is a plan: ask the
  person to confirm it still holds, listing what is tied to it.
- Whatever already reached someone is corrected by asking them, never edited behind their back. The correction says what
  changed, from what to what, and what you need from them.
- A move that leaves a record already right needs no question. Say nothing rather than ask for the sake of asking.
- When a fact was replaced, the newer fact is the latest word. A record that repeats the older one is pointed out in a
  note; one that is only close in wording is left.
- An inferred change is never made: if the move rests on something worked out and not said, leave the dates as they
  are and say so in a note.
