# Guide sections for the daily steward

Drafted with an outside assistant (2026-10-18) in the style of `engine/guide/guide.md`. That guide is what the
assistant answers questions from, and every line of it may be quoted as true, so a section moves into it only in the
change that builds its feature (phase S); until then it lives here. Each section answers what it does, what it never
does, and how to change it.

## Promises

The assistant keeps track of what you said you would do and what other people said they would do for you. Each promise
keeps its owner, its date if one was given, and the exact words it came from. A promise that depends on something else
keeps that condition: “if the budget is approved” does not become an unconditional deadline. A promise in quoted
history is not counted again.

A promise ends when the promised work is done or it is called off. A changed date updates the same promise; it does not
make another one. Part of the work arriving leaves the rest open. A reply that does not answer the question leaves the
question open. Passing the due date does not finish or delete anything. If the words do not make the owner, date or
outcome clear, the assistant asks rather than choosing for you.

Look over a promise in What changed and correct its owner, wording or date, or hand in a note explaining what happened.
You can mark it done or say why it is no longer needed. Undo reverses a change to the record and keeps both changes in
the history. It does not undo work already done outside the app.

## The morning brief

The morning brief brings together what changed since you last looked, what is at risk today and what needs your yes.
It draws on the mail, calendar and records the assistant has read. Promises you made and promises you are waiting for
both appear when they matter. On a quiet morning, it says that nothing needs you.

If today's to-dos need more time than your calendar leaves, the brief says how many minutes will not fit and
suggests what to move. Fixed items stay put. The brief never moves a meeting, changes a deadline or sends a follow-up.
If mail or calendar could not be read, it says so rather than presenting an incomplete day as clear.

Correct a wrong item where it was recorded, so the next brief uses the correction. Ask the assistant how to change the
brief's timing or what it includes. Where this install offers a setting, it proposes the change for you to review;
where it does not, it says so.

## Reminders and snooze

A reminder brings an item back at the time you chose. Snooze changes when you are reminded, not when the work is due.
You can choose in one hour, later today, this evening, tomorrow morning, next week, or pick a date and time. Choices
such as “this evening” use your saved times; if no clear time is available, you are asked to choose one.

Quiet hours hold ordinary reminders for a digest. A reminder that came due while this computer was asleep appears
when the app resumes, marked late, even during quiet hours. It is not dropped. For a repeating reminder, the app can
show each missed occurrence or one reminder listing them, according to your choice. A reminder email or calendar alarm
does not mean the underlying task has been done.

Choose another reminder time to snooze it, or change or remove the reminder without closing the task. Ask the assistant
how to change quiet hours, your usual snooze times or the way missed repeats are shown. Keep the task's due date unless
you mean to change that too.

## Bills

The assistant keeps a bill's due date and the reminders you choose before it. It can note an explicit date in a letter
or email, or use a repeating rule you give it. The words that supplied a date stay with it. Missing years, conflicting
dates and relative wording are shown as uncertain rather than turned into a made-up deadline.

It never connects to your bank, pays a bill or treats a passed due date as proof of payment. A reminder is not a payment
confirmation. It does not assume that a weekend or holiday changes the bill's terms.

Hand in a note to add or correct a bill's date, repeat rule or reminders. If a repeating rule needs a weekend or holiday
adjustment, say which way it should move. Record payment when you have paid, or say that the bill was cancelled; those
are different from snoozing its reminder.

## Bookings from confirmation emails

When a confirmation email includes booking details in a form the app can read, the assistant can pull out the booking's
dates, place, confirmation code and provider. This covers travel, lodging, events, restaurant bookings and rental cars.
Each fact stays with the confirmation it came from. A revised confirmation can propose a change to the existing booking
rather than adding a second copy.

It never invents a missing year, place or time zone, and it does not treat a hold or a proposed appointment as confirmed.
If a confirmation is incomplete or cannot be read, it says what is missing. Reading a confirmation never makes, changes
or cancels a booking with the provider. Adding it to your calendar needs your approval.

Look over the proposed booking in What changed. Correct a detail or hand in the updated confirmation. If you cancel with
the provider, record that cancellation or hand in its confirmation too. Removing a calendar entry does not cancel the
booking itself.

## Mail and calendar changes need your yes

The assistant can draft a reply or follow-up, suggest filing a message, or prepare a calendar change. Before anything
happens in your account, it shows the exact action: the message and recipients, the mail being moved or labeled, or the
calendar and event details being added, changed or removed. You can edit the proposal, approve it or leave it alone.

Connecting an account allows reading; it is not approval to send or change things. Your yes covers the action you saw,
not later changes to it. If a draft or its recipients change, it needs another look. If a calendar entry has changed
since the proposal was prepared, you see the new details before approving. Outside mail, attachments and add-ons cannot
give approval on your behalf.

Change the draft or decline the proposal before approving. If a connected service does not support the action, the app
says so; you can still copy a draft and send it yourself. To stop future access, sign out of the account in Settings.
Undoing a local record does not unsend an email or reverse a change already made in another service. Any correction
there is a new action for you to review.
