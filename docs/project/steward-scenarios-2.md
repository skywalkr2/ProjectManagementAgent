# A day with Thimbleby: more scenarios for phase S

These scenarios continue the first set in `steward-scenarios.md`. They cover bills, bookings, reminders and the morning brief. Rowan Vale (`rowan@example.com`) is an independent consultant; all other people, places and businesses below are fictional. Message addresses use `example.com`.

## Bills

16. **A due date in a statement.** Rowan has asked to be reminded two days before household bills are due. An email arrives from Meadowbrook Water (`billing@example.com`): “Your November water bill is $82.40. Payment is due on November 18, 2026. Your account reference is MW-1842.” Reginald proposes recording the bill and its due date, with the statement as evidence. On November 16, Today shows “Pay Meadowbrook Water · due November 18.” Never: pay the bill, connect a bank account, or change a date because it falls on a weekend or holiday.

17. **A revised amount.** Rowan already has a bill from Willow Heating (`accounts@example.com`) for $142.80, due December 2. A new email says: “Revised November statement: the amount due is $184.20, not $142.80. Payment is still due by December 2, 2026. Please use this statement in place of the earlier one.” Reginald shows the changed amount and both statements in What changed, then proposes updating the bill record. After Rowan approves, Today shows “Pay Willow Heating · due December 2” on the existing reminder date, with the revised amount. Never: pay it, hide the earlier figure, or guess which amount is correct if the revised message is unclear.

18. **A bill paid automatically.** Rowan’s open bill from Fernbank Power is due November 18. A receipt arrives from `receipts@example.com`: “We received your automatic payment of $63.10 on November 16, 2026 for account FP-903. Your November balance is paid.” Reginald shows the receipt in What changed and proposes marking that bill paid. After Rowan approves, the bill reminder no longer appears on November 16 or the due date. Never: initiate another payment, connect to a bank, or mark it paid based only on a scheduled debit or a past due date.

19. **A statement notice that is not a bill.** An email from Greenpath Living (`news@example.com`) says: “Your monthly statement is ready to view. Sign in to see your latest account news and offers.” It gives no amount, due date or payment request. Reginald leaves it as mail and creates no bill or payment reminder. Nothing appears on Today for it. Never: infer a balance or due date from the word “statement,” open an account link to look for one, or create a bill from a marketing notice.

## Bookings

20. **A lodging confirmation.** An email from Alder House (`stay@example.com`) to Rowan says: “Your reservation is confirmed. Guest: Rowan Vale. Check-in: November 21, 2026 after 3:00 p.m. Check-out: November 23, 2026 by 11:00 a.m. Location: 8 Alder Lane, Bellwether. Confirmation: AH-4821.” Reginald shows the dates, address and confirmation number in What changed. Today offers “Add Alder House stay to your calendar” for Rowan’s yes; once approved, the stay appears on the calendar for November 21–23. The email says all times are local to Alder House. Never: book anything with Alder House, fill in a missing detail, or add the event before Rowan approves it.

21. **A booking changes time.** Rowan’s calendar has dinner at Juniper Table on November 25 at 7:00 p.m. An email from `booking@example.com` says: “Juniper Table reservation JT-618 for Rowan Vale has moved from 7:00 p.m. to 7:45 p.m. on November 25, 2026. The date, party of two and table request are unchanged.” Reginald shows the old and new times in Changed overnight and offers “Move Juniper Table dinner to 7:45 p.m.” under Needs your yes. After Rowan approves, the calendar event changes. Never: send an acceptance, change the event before Rowan approves, or alter the date, party size or other details.

22. **A booking is canceled by its provider.** Rowan’s calendar contains a 10:00 a.m. ferry trip on December 4. The ferry office (`service@example.com`) emails: “We have canceled trip F-208 from Bellwether Pier on December 4, 2026 because the vessel is out of service. Your booking number is F-208.” Reginald shows the cancellation in Changed overnight and offers “Remove the December 4 ferry from your calendar” under Needs your yes. After Rowan approves, the local event is removed. Never: send a cancellation request for a trip the provider has already canceled, book another crossing, or remove the calendar entry without Rowan’s yes.

23. **A confirmed booking clashes with the calendar.** An email from Copper Kettle Café (`table@example.com`) says: “Your table for Rowan Vale and one guest is confirmed for 6:30 p.m. on December 9, 2026. Please arrive at 12 Mill Road, Bellwether. Booking CK-194.” Rowan’s calendar already has a client call from 6:00 to 7:00 p.m. that evening. Today shows the confirmed booking beside the overlapping client call and asks Rowan whether to keep it or draft a request for another time. Never: move or cancel either event, or contact the café without Rowan’s approval of the exact message.

## Reminders

24. **A reminder Rowan asked for.** On November 30, Rowan writes in a note: “Remind me at 9:00 a.m. Friday, December 4, to send Mira the revised estimate.” Reginald sets the reminder from Rowan’s clear instruction. At 9:00 a.m. on December 4, Today shows “Send Mira the revised estimate · due now” with the reminder choices. Never: send the estimate, tell Mira it is coming, or treat the reminder as proof that it was sent.

25. **A reminder tied to a meeting moves with it.** Rowan’s calendar has a consultation with Iona on December 3 at 2:00 p.m. Rowan asked: “Remind me 30 minutes before the consultation.” An updated invitation from `iona@example.com` says: “The consultation is moving to December 4, 2026 at 3:30 p.m. The length and location are unchanged.” Reginald shows the invitation change for review. After Rowan approves the event change, its linked reminder moves to 3:00 p.m. on December 4 and appears on Today then. Never: accept the invitation or change the event before Rowan approves; do not leave the linked reminder at the old time after an approved move.

26. **A reminder stops when the task is done.** Rowan has a reminder to return Ansel’s library book on December 7. On December 5, Rowan hands in this note: “I returned Ansel’s library book at lunch today.” Reginald marks the task done with Undo, and removes its future reminder. Nothing else is needed on Today for that task. Never: keep reminding Rowan after the note, send Ansel a message, or treat the note as permission to change a different promise.

27. **A reminder for Rowan to remind Mira.** Rowan writes: “Remind me Monday, December 7, at 9:00 a.m. to remind Mira to bring the sample to our review.” Reginald creates a reminder for Rowan. At the chosen time, Today says “Remind Mira to bring the sample · due now.” Never: notify Mira, add her to a reminder, or send a message on Rowan’s behalf.

## The morning brief

28. **A quiet day.** On Friday, December 11, Rowan’s connected mail and calendar have been read. There are no changes to report, nothing at risk and nothing waiting for Rowan’s yes. The brief says, on one line: “Nothing needs you this morning.” Never: fill the brief with empty sections, zero counts, status lines or cheerful filler.

29. **More work than the day can hold.** On Tuesday, November 24, a new client meeting takes 10:00 a.m. to noon; Rowan’s workday ends at 5:00 p.m. To-dos need eight hours, including a report Rowan promised by noon and a two-hour portfolio update due Friday. The portfolio update is not fixed and is the lowest-priority item. The brief says under Changed overnight: “A client meeting now takes 10:00 a.m. to noon.” Under At risk today: “Your to-dos need eight hours; your calendar has six free.” Under Needs your yes, it proposes: “Move the portfolio update to Wednesday.” Never: move the task or a meeting, change a client deadline, or promise a new date.

30. **The important item is waiting on someone else.** On Wednesday, December 2, Rowan’s brief is prepared after 10:00 a.m. The previous afternoon, Tavish (`tavish@example.com`) wrote: “I’ll send the final room measurements by 10:00 a.m. Wednesday, December 2. I’ll include the doorway width and ceiling height.” No measurements have arrived, and Rowan’s 11:00 a.m. planning call depends on them. The brief says under At risk today: “Tavish’s room measurements were due at 10:00 and have not arrived.” It adds: “Your planning call that needs them starts at 11.” Under Needs your yes, it offers a draft asking when he can send them. Never: mark the promise done, invent the measurements, send the draft or move the call without Rowan’s yes.

## Notes for turning them into tests

- Each arrival is evidence. A proposed change keeps its exact supporting words, and anything sent or changed outside the app waits for Rowan’s yes on that exact act.
- Scenario 18 uses a payment receipt from the biller, not a bank connection. Scenario 19 must not become a bill just because the message says “statement.”
- Scenario 25 moves the reminder only because Rowan tied it to the meeting. An independent reminder time does not move when a calendar event changes.
- Scenario 29 uses the capacity check: show the shortfall and propose a move, but do not move the task automatically.
